These are based on extensive research into BSc/Bachelor of Medical Laboratory Technology (MLT) / Medical Laboratory Science (MLS) programs (the actual target field; “MLT nursing” maps to this allied-health paramedical bachelor’s, typically 3–4 years including mandatory clinical internship/placement, distinct from pure nursing but frequently offered by the same institutions).  

Key research findings: Graduates work as Medical Laboratory Technologists/Scientists, performing diagnostic tests (hematology, microbiology, biochemistry, blood bank, etc.). In India, entry salaries are typically ₹2.5–4 LPA (fresher), rising to ₹6–10+ LPA with experience; abroad pathways (Gulf via Prometric/DataFlow, Australia via AIMS, Canada via CSMLS, US via ASCP, UK via HCPC/IBMS) offer substantially higher pay but require credential evaluation + licensing exams. “Placement” for this field is primarily the mandatory clinical internship + subsequent employment rate and salary outcomes (bottom quartile relative to local cost of living is the right metric, as official placement reports are often incomplete or gamed). Data sources include government university lists, accreditation bodies (NAACLS, AIMS, CSMLS, HCPC, etc.), placement reports, Numbeo CoL/QoL indices, visa/post-study work rules, and study-abroad platforms.

### Document 1: Phase 1 – Understanding the User and the Problem

**Primary User**  
- Indian family (brother is B.Tech student acting as researcher/advocate; sister is the applicant for a bachelor’s in MLT/MLS).  
- Likely 12th-pass with PCB (Physics, Chemistry, Biology), age ~17–20, seeking first undergraduate degree.  
- Constraints: Budget sensitivity (tuition + living + equipment + travel), preference for options that do not require immediate high travel for entrance exams, need for realistic international-student pathways, and long-term career mobility (ability to work/settle abroad after degree).  
- Decision style: Data-driven but overwhelmed by volume and opacity of information. Values transparent scoring, partial/estimated answers over “safe” but incomplete accuracy, and actionable next steps (past papers, registration deadlines, qualitative self-assessment).

**Core Problem**  
There is no generalized, continuously updated tool that scores *all* viable universities/programs worldwide on a consistent multi-dimensional basis tailored to MLT outcomes, while also filtering for real accessibility (exam feasibility, international friendliness, budget) for an Indian applicant. Existing search is fragmented across government portals, university sites, ranking lists (QS Life Sciences & Medicine, subject-specific), study-abroad platforms, and placement PDFs.  

Resulting pain points:  
- Incomplete or gamed outcome data (placement rates often exclude non-participants; salary distributions rarely publish bottom 25th percentile).  
- No standardized “quality of living at placement destination” adjustment (salary ÷ local CoL).  
- Admissions opacity: mix of merit, national/state entrance exams, university-specific tests, qualitative factors (extracurriculars, SOP, interviews). Many exams have closed registrations or require in-country appearance.  
- Budget opacity: tuition is published; equipment, hostel vs independent living, food/utilities, visas, and hidden costs are not.  
- International student experience and post-study work/PR pathways are rarely integrated into ranking.  
- Scale problem: thousands of programs; scraping the entire globe is wasteful and low-signal.  

**Missing Factors Identified in Research (beyond the original prompt)**  
These must be incorporated or the tool will produce misleading rankings:  
1. **Regulatory recognition & licensure pathway** – Critical for MLT. Degree must be acceptable to the target country’s regulator (ASCP/NAACLS for US, CSMLS for Canada, AIMS for Australia, HCPC/IBMS for UK, DHA/SCFHS etc. for Gulf, etc.). Bridging requirements or non-recognition can nullify outcomes.  
2. **Post-study work rights and PR pathways** – Length of post-study visa, shortage-list status for MLT/MLS, points for local study/experience. Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and (to a lesser extent) UK/Ireland/Germany score highly here; pure high-salary Gulf options may be temporary.  
3. **Language & cultural fit / safety for female students** – Beyond English tests (IELTS/OET). Safety indices, gender norms, support services, and real student-review NLP signals.  
4. **Clinical training quality** – Hours, hospital partnerships, equipment modernity, supervision ratios (not just “placement rate”).  
5. **Alumni mobility & long-term ROI** – Ability to move between countries or pursue further study (MSc, specialization).  
6. **Data quality & gaming resistance** – Explicit confidence intervals, source provenance, and flags for incomplete placement reports.  
7. **Visa rejection risk & international-student friendliness** – Acceptance rates for Indian students, proof-of-funds requirements, agent dependency.  
8. **Equipment & material costs specific to MLT** – Lab coats, kits, software, insurance (often under-reported).  
9. **Temporal dynamics** – Exam registration windows close; intake cycles; currency/fee changes; policy shifts (e.g., UK Graduate visa length changes, Australian age caps).  
10. **Mental-health / support infrastructure** and **part-time work rights** during study (affects net budget).

**User Jobs-to-be-Done**  
- “Show me the best realistic options I can actually get into and afford, ranked by expected early-career outcome adjusted for living costs and quality of life, with clear next actions.”  
- Secondary: compare hostel vs independent living, see past-paper links, self-score extracurriculars, filter closed/travel-required exams, understand country welcome for Indian students.

**Success Metrics for the Eventual Tool**  
- High coverage of high-signal countries/programs.  
- Transparent, reviewable scores with provenance.  
- Actionable outputs (deadlines, past papers, qualitative prompts, budget toggles).  
- Resistance to placement data gaming via bottom-quartile + CoL-adjusted metrics and confidence flags.
