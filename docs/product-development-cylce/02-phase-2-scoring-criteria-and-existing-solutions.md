**Document 2: Phase 2 – Solution Scoring Criteria + Existing Solutions Evaluation**  

These are based on extensive research into BSc/Bachelor of Medical Laboratory Technology (MLT) / Medical Laboratory Science (MLS) programs (the actual target field; “MLT nursing” maps to this allied-health paramedical bachelor’s, typically 3–4 years including mandatory clinical internship/placement, distinct from pure nursing but frequently offered by the same institutions).  

Key research findings: Graduates work as Medical Laboratory Technologists/Scientists, performing diagnostic tests (hematology, microbiology, biochemistry, blood bank, etc.). In India, entry salaries are typically ₹2.5–4 LPA (fresher), rising to ₹6–10+ LPA with experience; abroad pathways (Gulf via Prometric/DataFlow, Australia via AIMS, Canada via CSMLS, US via ASCP, UK via HCPC/IBMS) offer substantially higher pay but require credential evaluation + licensing exams. “Placement” for this field is primarily the mandatory clinical internship + subsequent employment rate and salary outcomes (bottom quartile relative to local cost of living is the right metric, as official placement reports are often incomplete or gamed). Data sources include government university lists, accreditation bodies (NAACLS, AIMS, CSMLS, HCPC, etc.), placement reports, Numbeo CoL/QoL indices, visa/post-study work rules, and study-abroad platforms.

### Document 2: Phase 2 – Scoring Criteria for Solutions + Evaluation of Existing Solutions

**Scoring Criteria for Any Solution Addressing This Problem** (weighted framework for evaluating tools/products)  

| Criterion | Weight | Description | Ideal Score Indicators |
|-----------|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| Outcome Depth (MLT-specific) | 25% | Placement/internship rate, salary distribution (esp. bottom 25th percentile), CoL-adjusted, employment quality, licensure pass rates | Publishes or estimates bottom-quartile salaries; adjusts for regional CoL; sources placement reports + NLP on reviews |
| Accessibility & Feasibility Filter | 20% | Exam requirements, registration status (live, not hardcoded), travel-required exams filtered, international-student friendliness, past-paper links | Dynamic deadline scraping; qualitative self-assessment prompts (1–5 extracurriculars etc.); country welcome scores |
| Budget Completeness | 15% | Tuition + equipment + living (hostel vs independent toggle) + food/utilities + misc + visa | Toggleable living options; regional CoL integration; confidence intervals |
| Data Freshness & Provenance | 15% | Scraping of government lists, rankings, placement PDFs, news; confidence scores; update cadence | Public data sources preferred; flags incomplete data; versioned scores |
| Personalization & Usability | 10% | User profile (marks, extracurriculars, budget, preferred regions), transparent scoring, top insights only | Multi-factor score with explanations; “top few insights that cover the picture” |
| Coverage Quality (not quantity) | 10% | Focused on high-signal countries/programs rather than exhaustive low-value scrape | Prioritizes top ~50 countries with program density + outcomes data |
| Extensibility & Reviewability | 5% | ML/NLP pipeline artifacts savable/reviewable; modular scoring | Saved intermediate data for audit |

**Existing Solutions Evaluated** (as many relevant as identified; scored roughly against the criteria above)  

- **General study-abroad platforms** (ApplyBoard, Edvoy, GradRight, Yocket, Shiksha, Leverage Edu, Bachelorsportal, Global Admissions, Admission Bravo, Applash, WiseAdmit, NextUni, StudyWhere AI, AdmissionsPedia, UniversiMedia): Strong on program search, basic fees, some comparison and AI matching. GradRight stands out for ROI/employment weighting. Weak on MLT-specific bottom-quartile salaries, dynamic exam deadlines, clinical outcome depth, and hostel-vs-independent toggles. Most are commission-influenced or surface-level.  
- **Ranking sites** (QS Life Sciences & Medicine, THE, subject-specific biomedical): Good academic/research signals; almost no placement salary distributions or accessibility filters.  
- **Country/specialty guides** (Hospital Careers healthcare study-abroad toolkit, UniversitySwitch, Tutopiya nursing/allied, various Indian education portals for BSc MLT): Useful qualitative insights on post-study work and recognition; not systematic scoring engines.  
- **Cost/QoL tools** (Numbeo, AdmissionsPedia city costs): Excellent CoL data that can be integrated; not university-program linked.  
- **Specialist MLT career pages** (ASCP, CSMLS, AIMS, HCPC, Gulf regulators, Indian NCAHP-related): Source of truth for licensure but not discovery/comparison tools.  
- **Gaps overall**: No existing tool combines deep MLT outcome mining (including partial estimates and gaming resistance), live exam-feasibility filtering, qualitative self-assessment, budget toggles, and focused high-signal country coverage with transparent reviewable ML insights. Closest approximates are GradRight (ROI) + AdmissionsPedia (costs + chance calculators) + specialized healthcare guides, but none deliver the full generalized pipeline requested.

**Conclusion of Phase 2**: A purpose-built tool is justified. Existing solutions cover discovery and basic comparison well but fail on the outcome-depth + accessibility + budget-completeness combination required for an Indian MLT applicant making a high-stakes decision.
